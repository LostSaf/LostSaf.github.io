MODULE 1 · BACTERIOLOGY

Naming: binomial Genus species. Classified by shape/arrangement + biochemical, physiological, genetic properties. Oral flora very diverse; shifts from aerobic pioneers → embedded anaerobic clusters. Names often hint at disease.

Cell Architecture & Envelope

Prokaryote: no nucleus/organelles; genome usually 1 un-segmented circular dsDNA in nucleoid; no introns, no mRNA splicing; transcription + translation simultaneous in one compartment rapid phenotype change under threat.

Eukaryote (host): double-membrane nucleus; linear chromosomes w/ introns; transcribe in nucleus → export → translate; membrane has sterols, mitochondria do ETC. Separation blocks bacterial DNA integrating into host transcription.

Host organelle sabotage: toxins punch holes in inner mito membrane → dissipate PMF, halt ATP → apoptosis · encapsulated pathogens arrest endosome maturation (lysosomal block) → replicate inside macrophages · secretion systems hijack actin → propel rods into neighbour cells. Early mechanical debridement stops spread through facial planes.

Ribosomes: bacteria 70S (50S+30S); human 80S (60S+40S) → selective toxicity. Clindamycin/erythromycin (macrolides) bind 50S, spare 80S.

Peptidoglycan (PG): mechanical + osmotic protection; backbone of alternating NAG–NAM (unique to prokaryotes, absent in humans); parallel chains wrap PM. Short peptide tails off NAM; transpeptidases (PBPs) covalently cross-link (D-Ala-D-Ala) → stability during growth. β-lactams (penicillin, amoxicillin) competitively + permanently bind transpeptidase → no cross-link → osmotic lysis.

Teichoic/LTA: negative charge; act as adhesins. LTA = inflammatory antigen; in pulp → macrophages → pulpitis/pain before bacteria reach pulp.

Periplasm (G−): fluid space between inner & outer membranes; concentrates β-lactamase destroys amoxicillin before it reaches targets.

Outer membrane: asymmetric — inner leaflet phospholipid, outer leaflet LPS; hydrophobic barrier.

LPS: O-antigen (outermost sugar; varies by mutation → disguise from antibodies in chronic infection) – core polysaccharide – Lipid A = endotoxin; shed mainly at cell death; binds TLR4 on macrophages inflammatory surge → osteoclasts → alveolar bone loss (periodontitis).

Porins: water-filled channels; passive diffusion of small water-soluble molecules; block large/charged. Narrower/fewer porins = resistance.

Capsule: organized water-insoluble polysaccharide/polypeptide; masks antigens; anti-phagocytic persists in tissue, survives transient bacteremia.

Slime layer/glycocalyx: loose unorganized EPS; sticks to enamel, epithelium, titanium implants, acrylic dentures; traps water (anti-desiccation). Antimicrobials penetrate poorly → mechanical scaling.

Flagella: helical flagellin filament; basal motor driven by proton-motive gradient; chemotaxis. Most oral strep lack flagella.

Axial filaments (endoflagella): in periplasm under outer sheath → corkscrew motility; Treponema denticola (anaerobic spirochete, red complex) drills through sulcus ECM → necrotizing periodontal ulceration.

Fimbriae: short rigid hair-like; adhesin tips bind host receptors → tissue tropism; initial irreversible attachment. Anti-plaque/anti-adhesive target.

Sex pili: longer, tubular, encoded by fertility (F) plasmids; hollow bridge → conjugation. Commensals pass β-lactamase genes to pathogens in plaque.

Endospores: dormant, dehydrated; form under starvation; cortex + keratin-like coat + (Ca) dipicolinic acid; survive boiling, chemicals, desiccation, radiation. Sporulation 6–8 h (asymmetric division in mother cell); germinate with water + nutrients. G+ rods Bacillus, Clostridium (C. difficile). Alcohol wipes fail; autoclave mandatory. Spores in non-vital anaerobic tissue (trauma/surgery) germinate → toxins.

Inclusions: non-membrane granules of carbon, phosphate (polyphosphate = high-energy P for ATP), glycogen. Oral strep store polysaccharide → lactic acid between meals → prolonged demineralization.

Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer, no sterols; selective transport; houses ETC + ATP synthase (no mitochondria). Chlorhexidine disrupts → leakage, lost potential, death.

Environment & Growth

Temp: mesophiles 20–45 °C (all oral pathogens, opt. 37 °C); cold ↓ membrane fluidity; heat denatures proteins/membranes. Fever impairs replication, ↑ neutrophil phagocytosis.

Autoclave: moist heat 121 °C, 15–30 min, 15 psi kills spores. Biological indicator: Geobacillus stearothermophilus spores, weekly spore testing.

pH: neutrophiles 6.5–7.5; aciduric grow < 5.0, pump protons out to stay neutral. Frequent sugar kills acid-sensitive commensals → S. mutans dominates.

Osmotic: hypertonic → water out → plasmolysis (cytoplasm shrinks from wall, no division); hypotonic → water in, rigid PG prevents lysis. Warm saline rinse (hypertonic) ↓ edema, limits surface bacteria.

Metabolism & Oxygen Ecology

Catabolism = exergonic breakdown → ATP; anabolism = endergonic synthesis; catabolism supplies carbon skeletons.

Glycolysis (Embden-Meyerhof): cytoplasm; 1 glucose (6C) → 2 pyruvate (3C) + net 2 ATP + 2 NADH; no O2. Main energy for cariogenic bacteria.

Fermentation: regenerates NAD⁺ w/o external e⁻ acceptor. Homolactic → lactic acid only (S. mutans); heterolactic → lactic + ethanol + acetic acid. Critical pH 5.5 → hydroxyapatite dissolves.

TCA (Krebs): pyruvate → acetyl-CoA → GTP, NADH, FADH2, CO2. ETC in plasma membrane pumps H⁺ out → PMF → ATP synthase, up to 34 ATP/glucose. Membrane-depolarizing agents halt ATP instantly. Aerobic commensals win on oxygenated keratinized surfaces.

Anaerobic respiration: nitrate/sulfate acceptors; ATP: fermentation < anaerobic resp. < aerobic. Deep pockets → volatile sulfur compounds (malodor, tissue damage).

O2 classes: obligate aerobe (Mycobacterium) · obligate anaerobe, killed by O2/ROS (P. gingivalis) · facultative (Streptococcus). Supragingival facultatives use up O2 → anoxia → anaerobes colonize.

ROS: superoxide (O2⁻), H2O2. SOD: O2⁻ → O2 + H2O2; catalase/peroxidase: H2O2 → H2O + O2. Obligate anaerobes lack SOD + catalase → O2-releasing agents/debridement hurt them.

Necrotic pulp: sealed, non-vital, blood supply gone → highly reduced → proteolytic obligate anaerobes acute periapical abscess. Pocket gradient: healthy sulcus = high positive redox (fluid flow); deep pocket = stagnant, reduced → anaerobic selection zone.

Caries chain: saccharolytic bacteria → glycolysis (O2-independent) → pyruvate reduced to lactic acid pumped into plaque → pH < 5.5.

Stephan curve: glucose rinse → sharp pH drop within 5 min; < 5.5 fluid unsaturated → enamel dissolves; recovery over 30–40 min by salivary flow + bicarbonate (HCO3⁻). Xerostomia → prolonged acid, ↑ caries.

Syntrophy: Veillonella cannot ferment sugar; eats lactic acid → weaker propionic/acetic acids → stabilizes pH, protects enamel.

Protein catabolism (deep pockets): asaccharolytic/proteolytic bacteria lack sugar enzymes → use GCF & blood (albumin, transferrin, hemin); secrete proteases (gingipains, collagenases) cleaving collagen, transferrin, IgG, sIgA → peptides imported → deamination → ammonia → pocket pH > 7.5 (optimizes gingipains) bone loss; independent of dietary sugar (sugar restriction won't stop it).

VSCs: from sulfur amino acids → break down connective tissue; main cause of halitosis.

Iron: pathogens need Fe³⁺ (ETC, DNA). Nutritional immunity: host locks iron in transferrin, ferritin, lactoferrin; bacteria secrete siderophores. Iron overload → faster fascial infections.

Folate: bacteria can't absorb folate, make THF from PABA; humans absorb dietary folate sulfonamides = selective.

Growth Curve

Binary fission: FtsZ ring at mid-cell septum; autolysins break wall bonds to insert new NAG/NAM. β-lactams kill actively dividing cells, not dormant ones.

Lag: no division, make enzymes/ribosomes/ATP · Log: max constant rate; most sensitive to cell-wall Abx · Stationary: division = death; dormancy (↓ metabolism, thicker walls, stress genes) → ↓ β-lactam effect · Death: exponential lysis. Chronic abscess (stationary) needs drainage/debridement, not Abx alone.

Genetics, Resistance & Stewardship

Chromosome: single circular dsDNA in nucleoid. Plasmids: small circular, replicate independently; accessory genes (resistance, exotoxins).

Horizontal gene transfer: Transformation = uptake of naked DNA from lysed cells (competent oral strep) · Transduction = bacteriophage carries DNA · Conjugation = contact-dependent plasmid transfer via sex pilus; relaxosome nicks plasmid, 1 strand passes (rolling-circle), F⁻ → F⁺. Main route of MDR spread in plaque; viridans strep & E. faecalis = resistance reservoirs.

Oral Biofilms

Biofilm: community in EPS (polysaccharide + enzymes + eDNA + host lipids) with water channels. Tolerates Abx up to 1000×; matrix blocks charged antimicrobials & antibodies, holds β-lactamases, metabolic cooperation, buffers environment. Mechanical disruption (SRP) must precede/accompany chemicals.

Acquired enamel pellicle: acellular salivary glycoproteins, statherins, proline-rich proteins, phosphoproteins; forms within minutes of cleaning; protects from attrition but provides receptors. Antigen I/II strep adhesins bind salivary agglutinins.

Stages: pellicle → reversible attachment (electrostatic/van der Waals) → irreversible adhesin-receptor + EPS secretion → maturation (secondary colonizers bind pioneers). Pellicle re-forms right after polishing → daily mechanical removal.

Pioneers: S. mitis, S. oralis; bind pellicle, consume O2, lower redox; don't damage enamel directly.

Co-aggregation: cell-to-cell binding of secondary colonizers. F. nucleatum = bridge (early G+ strep ↔ late G− anaerobes) → succession; supplies H2S + amino acids to P. gingivalis (synergy). Polymicrobial disease.

Supragingival plaque: coronal, saliva, high O2, dietary carbs; strep + Actinomyces scaffold; "corncob" = cocci around central filament (Corynebacterium/Fusobacterium); glucan/fructan EPS blocks bicarbonate → smooth-surface caries.

Subgingival plaque: apical, anaerobic, GCF-fed; "test-tube brush" = central filaments + perpendicular motile G− rods. Tooth-attached layer: dense calcifying rods on cementum. Epithelium-associated: loose, most virulent (motile rods, spirochetes, cocci) invades sulcular epithelium.

Calculus: mineralized plaque (Ca phosphate; brushite → hydroxyapatite by heterogeneous nucleation); supra minerals from saliva, sub from GCF; inside dead, rough living surface. Not directly inflammatory — nidus for plaque → scale.

Quorum sensing: autoinducers rise with density → threshold → group gene expression (virulence, matrix). Target w/o resistance.

Sucrose: glucose + fructose. GTFs → insoluble glucans (matrix cement); FTFs → fructans (labile energy store). Sucrose uniquely cariogenic.

Diagnostics

Sampling: pus aspiration (abscess), paper points (pocket). Blood agar (enriched non-selective) vs selective/differential. Anaerobes need sealed vials w/ O2-reducing agents O2 exposure → false negatives. Culture + sensitivity for refractory/spreading infection.

PCR: amplifies species-specific 16S rRNA genes in hours; > 50% oral bacteria unculturable.

Gram stain: crystal violet + iodine kept by thick PG (G+ purple); alcohol washes out thin wall (G− pink w/ safranin). Darkfield (oblique light, live motile spirochetes bright) · phase-contrast (refractive index, wet mounts) instant spirochete confirmation in ANUG.

MODULE 2 · PATHOGENESIS & HOST DEFENSES

Immunology Foundations

Innate: immediate, non-specific, from birth, no memory (holding action). Adaptive: vertebrate-only, triggered by innate signals, memory.

1st line: intact skin/mucosa, ciliated cells, flushing; lysozyme (saliva, tears, GCF) cleaves PG. Extractions breach it. Humoral innate: antimicrobial peptides (disrupt membranes); lactoferrin/transferrin (bind iron); salivary peroxidase (ROS). Xerostomia → rampant root caries within months.

Complement: serum cascade — opsonization (C3b), leukocyte recruitment, lysis by MAC. Early-component deficiency → recurrent pyogenic infections, bacteremia.

Acute inflammation: redness, heat, swelling, pain, loss of function. Mast cells release histamine (+ heparin) → vasodilation, plasma exudate (edema) pressure → pulpal pain. Gingivitis protective; chronic → bone loss.

PRRs (e.g., TLRs) detect PAMPs (LPS, PG, flagellin) → pro-inflammatory signal. TLR polymorphisms → aggressive periodontal destruction.

PMNs: most abundant WBC; first responders via diapedesis. Phagocytosis: chemotaxis → adherence (opsonins) → ingestion (phagosome) → digestion (phagolysosome); degranulation, oxidative burst. Constant sulcus migration protects healthy gingiva.

Macrophages: from monocytes/tissue precursors; sustained defense, debris; APCs present antigen on MHC. Chronic activation → MMPs → collagen loss, bone loss.

Cytokines: pro-inflammatory IL-1, TNF-α, IL-6 (fever, acute-phase proteins, recruitment); anti-inflammatory IL-10, TGF-β. ↑ TNF-α + IL-1 → osteoclasts → alveolar bone resorption.

Type I IFN (α, β): from virus-infected cells → neighbours enter antiviral state. Holds HSV before CTLs arrive.

Antibodies: Fab = variable antigen-binding; Fc = constant effector.
IgG most abundant serum (+GCF), high affinity, crosses placenta, secondary response, opsonizes/complement · IgM pentamer, first responder, low affinity/high avidity, best complement activator · IgA/sIgA dimer in saliva/mucosa, blocks adhesion (IgA deficiency → mucosal infections, early caries) · IgE type I hypersensitivity, worms. Affinity maturation: IgM → high-affinity IgG.

CD4⁺ TH ("generals"): cytokines activate B cells (class switching) + macrophages. CD8⁺ CTL: perforin + granzymes → apoptosis of infected cells. NK: innate; kill cells with ↓ MHC I ("missing self"). HIV kills CD4⁺ → oral candidiasis.

Multi-Kingdom Pathogenesis

Pathogenicity = qualitative (yes/no); virulence = quantitative degree, via virulence factors. Infection (disease) vs colonization (no symptoms).

Direct damage = microbial enzymes/toxins/subversion. Indirect = host's own response (cytokines, lysosomal enzymes, prostaglandins). Periodontal destruction mainly indirect → host-modulating drugs help.

Adhesion: non-specific (van der Waals, hydrophobic) → irreversible adhesin-receptor; tropism = matching receptors.

Invasins: hyaluronidase (dissolves "cellular cement" → cellulitis spreads through fascia); collagenase (P. gingivalis destroys PDL → mobility).

Exotoxins: proteins secreted by live bacteria (tetanus, diphtheria; necrosis, toxemia). Endotoxin (LPS/lipid A): G− outer membrane, released at lysis, TLR4. LPS left in dentinal tubules → endo failure.

T3SS: G− "molecular syringe" injects effectors → hijack actin, collapse mito PMF (apoptosis), block phagolysosome maturation.

Viruses: spread horizontal (droplet, aerosol, contact) or vertical. Latency (HSV, VZV, EBV; recur from ganglia w/ stress/UV) vs chronic replication (HBV, HIV). Integration (retroviruses, oncogenic) → transformation.

Fungi: Candida albicans, opportunistic; dimorphism yeast ↔ invasive hyphae; SAPs (aspartyl proteinases) penetrate mucosa. Broad-spectrum Abx kill protective bacteria → overgrowth.

Parasites: protozoa (trophozoite ↔ cyst; cysts survive chlorination → filter); helminths (obstruction, malabsorption).

Prions: protein only; PrPSc converts PrPC α-helix → β-sheet. Survive 121 °C autoclave → NaOH + high-temp steam.

Evasion: capsule/biofilm shielding; antigenic variation (viruses, protozoa → yearly flu shot); host mimicry; intracellular sanctuary (block phagolysosome); MHC I interference; cytokine/IFN subversion intracellular → need cell-penetrating drugs.

Persistence: M. leprae 5–20 yr incubation; post-infectious sequelae (rheumatic fever). Spread: fascial planes (least resistance), bacteremia Ludwig angina = airway obstruction.

Host risk: diabetes (hyperglycemia ↓ PMN chemotaxis, adhesion, oxidative burst; poor collagen healing) · immunosuppression (cyclosporine ↓ T-cells; biologics/steroids ↓ macrophages) · age/hormones (immunosenescence; puberty/pregnancy/menopause → pregnancy gingivitis) · genetics (cytokine/complement polymorphisms → tailored recall).

Opportunistic: commensal → disease if defenses fail, flora displaced or barrier breached viridans strep → IE. Bacteriostatic only halts growth → needs host immunity (fails in neutropenia).

Disease Causation & Evidence

Koch (1890): ① microbe in all diseased, not healthy ② isolate in pure culture ③ causes disease in healthy host ④ re-isolate identical. Fails in dentistry: polymicrobial; "pathogens" found at healthy sites.

Gnotobiotic = germ-free animal + 1 strain (isolates 1 species' potential; proved caries infectious + transmissible; not polymicrobial) · cell culture = human lines (cytokines, toxicity, epithelial breach; no animals) · cross-sectional = diseased vs healthy at 1 time (associations, no cause/effect) · longitudinal = same pts over time (gold standard; showed environment shifts precede disease; costly, needs compliance).

MODULE 3 · VIROLOGY

Architecture, Classification & Replication

Viruses: acellular; no ribosomes/ATP; obligate intracellular. Virion = complete infectious extracellular particle; DNA or RNA, never both. Size: parvovirus 20 nm → pox 300–400 nm; pass 0.22 µm filters; need EM; millions/mL saliva/blood. Masks capture electrostatically, not by straining → aerosols need fitted N95.

Capsid of capsomeres: icosahedral (20 faces, 12 vertices; adenovirus, herpes) or helical (influenza, rabies). Repetitive epitopes = PAMPs → strong B-cell response w/o adjuvant.

Envelope: host lipid (plasma/nuclear/ER) gained at egress + viral glycoprotein spikes (influenza HA, SARS-CoV-2 spike) for attachment + fusion. Lose envelope = inactive.

1 Attachment: VAP ↔ receptor. Cell tropism (EBV → CD21 B cells; rabies → ACh receptor); host range narrow (smallpox, polio) vs broad (rabies, West Nile, SARS-CoV-2); coreceptors (HIV: CD4 + CCR5/CXCR4). SARS-CoV-2 binds ACE2 on oral epithelium + salivary ducts.

4 Synthesis: early = enzymes, regulators, evasion proteins → genome replication → late = structural proteins; host shutoff. Antivirals hit early enzymes.

5–6 Assembly/egress: spontaneous; naked → lysis; enveloped → bud (PM or trans-Golgi); protease maturation (HIV, HCV) → target of PIs.

One-step growth: eclipse (uncoating → first intracellular infectious virion; none detectable) · latent (infection → extracellular release) · burst, 100 → >10,000 virions/cell (vs bacterial doubling) abrupt onset of flu/COVID.

Pathogenesis, Host Defense & Antivirals

CPE: metabolic hijacking; membrane failure → lysis; inclusions: Cowdry A (nuclear, HSV), Negri (cytoplasmic, rabies) giant cells + Cowdry A on smear = HSV. Syncytia: fused neighbours → spread hidden from antibodies (HSV, VZV).

HPV 16/18 oncogenesis: E6 degrades p53 (ubiquitin-proteasome; no repair/apoptosis); E7 binds pRb → frees E2F → S-phase. OPSCC in tonsillar crypts, base of tongue.

Sensing: endosomal TLR3 dsRNA · TLR7/8 ssRNA · TLR9 unmethylated CpG DNA; cytosolic RIG-I/MDA5 (5′-triphosphate RNA, dsRNA). TLR3 defects → HSV encephalitis.

Type I IFN: paracrine → antiviral state (block protein synthesis, degrade RNA); causes prodromal fever, chills, aches.

CD8⁺ CTL: read viral peptides on MHC I → perforin/granzyme. Keeps EBV latent in B cells; failure → oral hairy leukoplakia (lateral tongue). Antibodies neutralize free virions; sIgA blocks coxsackie attachment (herpangina).

Evasion: ↓ MHC I (NK backup kills "missing self"); IFN blockade; antigenic variation. CMV degrades MHC I → silent salivary shedding.

HBV particles: enveloped partial dsDNA; Dane particle = 42 nm infectious virion; 22 nm decoy spheres/filaments of pure HBsAg outnumber it up to 100,000:1, soak up antibodies basis of recombinant HBsAg vaccine.

Superinfection: ruptured ulcers (HSV, coxsackie) let bacteria in; flu/RSV kill ciliated cells → 2° pneumonia; HIV → thrush; measles hits memory lymphocytes → transient immunosuppression. Impetiginized blisters need antibacterials.

Oral reservoir: salivary glands (mumps, CMV, EBV, rabies); GCF carries HBV, HCV, HIV; mucosal receptors (SARS-CoV-2, coxsackie); silent shedding. Mumps: bilateral parotitis, sheds before swelling.

Oral sentinels: Measles — Koplik spots (bluish-white macules, red buccal mucosa opposite molars; 24–48 h before rash) · Coxsackie — herpangina (vesicles soft palate/tonsillar pillars), HFMD (anterior oral ulcers + palms/soles) · Rubella — Forchheimer spots (soft-palate petechiae, prodrome).

Antiviral principles: target viral-specific enzymes; virostatic (can't clear latent/integrated); narrow safety margin; prodrugs activated by viral enzymes. Coxsackie/HFMD: supportive only.

Acyclovir: guanosine analog w/o chain-extension link (3′-OH); 1st phosphate by viral thymidine kinase, then host kinases → chain terminator. Valacyclovir = ester prodrug, better oral absorption; ganciclovir for CMV. Useless without viral TK (HPV, coxsackie, measles); for HSV/VZV.

HBV 10× HCV, 100× HIV. HBV serology: HBsAg = active infection, infectious · anti-HBs = immune (vaccine or recovered) · anti-HBc = natural infection past/present, never from vaccine · HBeAg = high replication/infectivity. Anti-HBs ≥ 10 mIU/mL + anti-HBc⁻ = vaccine immunity. Documented titres mandatory. HBV spreads w/o visible blood.

HDV: smallest; circular RNA coding only HD antigen; needs HBV's HBsAg coat. Coinfection → severe acute, <5% chronic; superinfection of chronic HBV → fulminant, cirrhosis 70–80%. HBV vaccine protects vs HDV.

HCV (env. (+)ssRNA, Flaviviridae): constant variants → no vaccine; DAAs 8–12 wk, no interferon; anti-HCV = exposure, RNA PCR = active PCR tells cured from viremic.

HIV: 2 RNA copies + RT, integrase, protease; gp120 binds CD4 (TH, macrophages, dendritic cells), coreceptor CCR5; gp41 fusion. CCR5 Δ32/Δ32 → natural resistance. Acute retroviral (2–4 wk, flu-like) → clinical latency 8–10 yr → AIDS: CD4 < 200/µL (normal 500–1500) or AIDS-defining illness. Oral signs: pseudomembranous candidiasis, OHL (EBV), Kaposi sarcoma (HHV-8). ART → < 20–50 copies/mL, CD4 rebounds; U=U; routine care, standard precautions (refusing care = unethical).

PEP protocol: ① wash wound w/ soap + water, flush mucosa w/ saline; don't squeeze/scrub ② report to supervisor ③ test source (HBV/HCV/HIV) with consent ④ HIV PEP ASAP — ideally < 2 h, max 72 h ⑤ 28-day, 3-drug ART. HBV: HBIG + vaccine only if non-immune.

Solo practice (2,000 pts): HBV ~680/100k (~50% undiagnosed) → seen every 2 wk; HCV ~470/100k (~44% unaware) → every 2.5–3 wk; HIV ~170/100k (~11% unaware) → every 7 wk; HDV ~20/100k → every 1.5–2 yr. Infected patient weekly, usually unknown.

Other Oral Viruses, Vaccines, Aerobiology

Herpesviridae (env. dsDNA, lifelong latency): α neurotropic (sensory ganglia) — HSV-1/2: trigeminal ganglion; primary gingivostomatitis, herpes labialis, herpetic whitlow; VZV: dorsal root/cranial ganglia; chickenpox → zoster, Ramsay Hunt · β lymphotropic — CMV (salivary glands, deep ulcers, congenital), HHV-6/7 · γ oncogenic — EBV (memory B cells; mono, OHL, Burkitt), HHV-8 (Kaposi sarcoma). Dental trauma can reactivate; zoster = unilateral, dermatomal, stops at midline.

Respiratory: influenza A/B, SARS-CoV-2 (ageusia), RSV, rhino, adeno; droplet nuclei + fomites. Influenza drift = point mutations; shift = segment reassortment. Handpieces/ultrasonics aerosolize high-titre saliva → HVE at tip + fit-tested respirators.

HPV: HPV-OPSCC rising in young non-smokers; HPV-16 > 85%; hidden in tonsillar crypts/base of tongue (unlike visible tobacco leukoplakia). Low-risk 6, 11, 13, 32 → squamous papilloma, Heck disease. Look for unilateral tonsil asymmetry, firm painless neck node.

Vaccines: live-attenuated (sIgA + IgG + CD8 memory; MMR, varicella; contraindicated in severe immunocompromise) · inactivated (adjuvant/boosters, mainly IgG; IPV, rabies) · subunit/VLP (safe, antibody-driven; HBV, HPV) · mRNA (LNP; humoral + cellular; SARS-CoV-2).

Staff immunization: HBV + titre; annual flu; MMR ×2 or serology; VZV vaccine or IgG; annual COVID; 9-valent HPV herd immunity.

Aerobiology: aerosol < 5 µm (airborne for hours, reaches alveoli) · 5–50 µm (settles 10–30 min) · splatter > 50 µm (1–2 m, lands on surfaces/face/eyes). Sources: ultrasonic scalers, high-speed handpieces, air-water syringe.

Disinfection: Spaulding — touch surfaces = non-critical → intermediate-level. Susceptibility: enveloped viruses (easiest) → vegetative bacteria, fungi → naked viruses + spores (hardest). Tuberculocidal kills naked viruses too.

IPAC layers: ① PPE (N95, eye shield, gown) ② HVE + pre-procedural rinse ③ barriers + tuberculocidal disinfectant ④ HBV immunization + PEP.
